# ✦ Classic Game Web System Ideas

Three possible directions for transforming the classic-game dataset into an engaging web
experience.

---

## 🎮 01 · Classic Game Information Hub

### The Vision

A polished reference library for classic games—part digital museum, part game database, and
part IMDb-style archive.

### What It Could Offer

- Rich profiles with descriptions, platforms, dates, genres, developers, publishers, ratings,
  and images
- Easy browsing through gaming history and different eras
- Historical context for important games and platforms
- Practical guides for people discovering older games
- A nostalgic space for collectors, families, players, and developers

---

## ✧ 02 · Discovery & Recommendation System

### The Vision

A smart search experience that helps users find classic games they will enjoy, even when they
do not know the title they are looking for.

### What It Could Offer

- Search by title, platform, genre, decade, developer, publisher, or rating
- Filters that make a large collection simple to explore
- Search by how many copies a game have sold
- Recommendations based on similar genres, tags, platforms, or ratings
- Timeline browsing for discovering games by release period
- Clear game pages focused on the most useful information

### Best Suited For

Players who want to discover classic games but need help knowing where to start.

---

## ✦ 03 · Interactive Classic-Game Library

### The Vision

A visually memorable library that turns browsing into an experience. Games could be arranged
by platform, genre, decade, or developer.

### Signature Interaction

Floating category bubbles could invite users to explore areas such as:

`ARCADE` · `1990s` · `SEGA` · `ACTION` · `HIGHLY RATED`

### Library Experience

- Organized sections with clean game grids
- Short introductions for every category
- Fast filters and simple navigation
- Links to complete game profiles
- A creative visual entrance supported by a practical library structure

---

## ◈ Shared Strengths

Every direction can make use of the dataset's:

**13,603 games** · **14 platforms** · release history · genres and tags · developers and
publishers · ratings · descriptions · screenshots and other image assets

The strongest experiences will acknowledge that ratings and images are not available for every
game, coverage is uneven across platforms and decades, and some labels may need cleaning or
standardization.

These cards present a range of possibilities without selecting a final project.
